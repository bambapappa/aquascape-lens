# Research notes: what aquascapers need when they plan a layout

Written before the plan-lens interview, 2026-10-22. These notes are the
evidence behind the answers given in `design-brief.md`. They are a summary of
public aquascaping guidance, not original research.

## The problem the Lens targets

- **Hardscape is placed dry, before water.** Rearranging stones and wood in a
  filled tank clouds the water for days and uproots plants, so aquascapers do
  the composition in an empty tank and photograph it from several angles
  (front, both sides, 45 degrees) before committing. Professionals spend hours
  or days on this stage. Sources: Gensou, "Hardscape Dry Layout Tips" and
  "How to Plan Your Aquarium Hardscape Before Adding Water".
- **Scale is the beginner mistake that cannot be fixed later.** Stones that
  are too small for the tank height leave the upper half empty, and no plant
  can compensate. Green Aqua's guidance is that the hardscape should reach
  roughly 40 to 60 percent of the tank height; Iwagumi guides put the main
  stone (Oyaishi) at about two thirds of the water height.
- **Composition rules are simple but easy to break while your hands are
  busy.** Focal point one third in from a side, never centred. Odd numbers of
  main elements (3, 5, 7). One dominant material. Three depth planes
  (foreground, midground, background). A gap of a few centimetres between the
  hardscape and the front glass for cleaning. These come from Green Aqua's
  "How to Design Your Aquascape Like a Professional" and "Mastering Iwagumi",
  and are repeated by Gensou, Fishyhub and Horizon Aquatics.
- **Plant heights by zone.** Foreground 0 to 5 cm (carpets such as Monte
  Carlo), midground 5 to 20 cm (Cryptocoryne, small bushes), background 15 cm
  and up (stem plants such as Rotala). Sources: Gensou, Fishyhub, Garnelio.
- **Existing tools.** Paper sketches, photos of the empty tank, and one iPad
  app (Aquarium Planner AR) that places a whole tank in a room. Nothing lets
  you place individual stones and plants at real size inside the tank you are
  standing in front of while your hands stay free to move the real pieces.

## Why this is a glasses app and not a phone app

- **Shares your view.** The guides (thirds grid, zones, focal point) are drawn
  on the tank you are looking at, from where you stand.
- **Draws in the world.** Stones, wood and plants appear at real size inside
  the tank, so the scale question is answered by looking, not by measuring.
- **Leaves your body free.** Both hands stay on the real stones. You pinch
  once to swap a virtual piece, then keep working.

## Numbers used in the brief

| Item | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Reference tank | 60 x 30 x 36 cm (W x D x H), a standard "60P" | common nano/standard size |
| Table height | 75 cm | typical aquarium stand |
| Focal point band | 28 to 45 percent of width from one side | Green Aqua rule GA-03 |
| Hardscape height | at least 40 percent of tank height | Green Aqua rule GA-07 |
| Front glass gap | at least 3 cm | Green Aqua rule GA-13 |
| Main elements | odd count | Green Aqua rules GA-05, GA-18 |
| Stone size ladder | 1.0, 0.6, 0.4 of the main stone | Iwagumi guides (Gensou, Fishyhub) |
| Plant zones | fg 0-5 cm, mg 5-20 cm, bg 15 cm+ | Gensou, Fishyhub, Garnelio |
